alone, and 4 read-only investigators doing reconnaissance for P22–P25 — plus 16 follow-up rounds.

wave boundary were the orchestrator's own five spine edits.

## 3. What the audit found that the gate did not

The gate was green at roughly 390 new tests when the auditors started.

### Two CRITICAL

**C-03 — figure extraction could be disabled and 75 tests stayed green.** Wrapping `DIGIT_RUN` in
`\b` failed **0 of 75** tests, measured. The no-boundaries rule was _documented_ and _tested
nowhere_. The mechanism is worse than a missed match: `\b` sits between neither `0`/`g` nor `a`/`3`,
and backtracking `\d+` to `2` fails identically — so `20g` yields **no figure at all**, check 3 has
nothing to compare, and an invented number is **contained by default**. `540kcal` and `20g` are the
ordinary way a model writes a nutrition figure, so the mutation disabled the common path, not an edge
case. Closed by three rows, one covering a leading boundary the others miss.

**C-01 and C-02 are P23's**, found by this window's reconnaissance and recorded in full there: the
contrast guard is still vacuously satisfiable at the `ComponentTokens` layer (68 of 79 colour members
measured by nothing), and `home-preferences-recovered` — the notice saying the user's allergy list
was emptied — is announced to nobody.

### Eight assertions that no production change could fail

A dedicated vacuity hunter found eight, by mutating **out-of-tree replicas** rather than the
repository. All eight are closed, and the shape of the result matters:

**Five needed no production change at all.** `promptSafety.ts`, `chatFormat.ts`, `provider.ts`,
`containment.ts` (for the per-occurrence finding) and `prompt.ts` are byte-identical to before their
fix rounds, same recorded hashes. In each case the code was already correct and the module's own
docstring already stated the property; what was missing was any test that would notice it stopping.

**So the honest headline is that P19 shipped correct code with under-built evidence** — not defects.
C-03 is the exception worth naming separately, because its loss would have reached a user.

The most instructive of the eight, each in one line:

- `chatFormat.ts` — `minItems: 1` on the empty branch fails nothing, and would make **every count
  question a deterministic 503**, because TSD §4.9 gives a `count` answer an empty `namedMeals`.
- `provider.ts` — forwarding `generation` whole compiles and leaves the request body byte-identical,
  so no fixture reachable from the seam's own parameters could see it. Closed with a pass-through
  recorder wrapped around the **real** client factory.
- `prompt.ts` — `FIGURES` was pinned by `toBe('10.10 12.75')` on a fixture whose figures were already
  clean, so `neutraliseUntrusted` was a **no-op on that string**: deleting the call changed no byte
  any assertion read, and the old probe reddened on the _join_.
- `promptSafety.ts` — the three-stage pipeline was pinned at one of its **two** joints. Swapping
  strip and collapse changed 0 of 58 inputs, and on an input interleaving zero-width characters
  between angle brackets the swap emits `<<<[redacted]>>>` — the fence markers' own shape.

### One shipped defect, deterministic, in the catalog

`meals.json` carries both `English Breakfast` and `Full English Breakfast`. Check 4 forbids any
catalog name absent from the prompt, and the shorter occurs on a word boundary inside the longer —
so **every answer about that dish returned 503, at temperature 0, forever.** One meal in sixty the
assistant could never discuss.

Repaired with TSD §4.9's own longest-first claiming mechanism. **The mechanism then turned out to be
wrong in four independent ways, each found by a different reader after the previous fix looked
complete:**

1. the orchestrator's abuse argument missed a **straddling** name (`Chicken Tikka Masala` +
   `Masala Dosa`, answer `"Chicken Tikka Masala Dosa"`), so blanking had to become a **coverage**
   predicate;
2. the search examined only the **first occurrence** of each forbidden name;
3. the predicate at the **claiming guard** was independently mutable — `overlaps` → `covers` flipped
   a verdict with 428/428 green;
4. `digitsIn` → `quotedFigures` loosened check 3 with 428/428 green, and the existing fixture could
   not discriminate because `7-Spice Chicken` carries a **digit** rather than spelling a cardinal.

Each now has its own fixture. The lesson is in the file: **every part of that exemption needs its own
oracle, because each part is independently mutable and the others rescue it.**

### One safety gap closed under PRD authority

`"You should not eat the fish"` passed containment check 2, and so did `"the safest choice for you"`.
Whole-word matching on `" you should eat "` cannot see the first, and TSD §5.7 spells out three forms
of `healthy` and only one of `safe`.

Six extensions were added under **PRD §7.3** — which outranks TSD §5.7 and forbids medical or dietary
advice — each traceable to a rule §5.7 states itself ("with their inflections", "negation is not an
exemption"). `DOCUMENTED_CLAIMS` stays pinned to a literal transcription of the sixteen, separately
from `PRD_EXTENSIONS`, so a drift in either is caught alone.

**And the class problem is recorded as R-64 rather than papered over:** the list grew 16 → 19 → 22
across three passes with **no new concept**, so it cannot be completed by inspection. Five known
gaps were deliberately **not** added, because adding words by hand while reporting that the approach
does not scale would be incoherent.

## 4. Probes — and an honest correction about their counts

**73 probes were run across the writer rounds, plus 17 by the mutation auditor and 23 more in the
final containment rounds.** Every one was restored and hash-verified.

**The failure counts in the writers' reports are indicative, not exact.** One agent found that
batched probe runs on this machine give inconsistent totals — one probe read 6 failures batched and 2
alone, three others read 2 batched and 1 alone. Failing test **names** and restore hashes were
correct every time; only the totals moved. It labelled its own earlier numbers indicative and
re-verified nine individually.

So the evidence in this report is the **failing test names, the assertion messages, and the
byte-identical restores** — not the counts. The fleet brief now requires one mutation per run.

### The mutation auditor's restore statement

It ran **alone**, with no other agent in the tree, which is the only basis for its licence to mutate
files it does not own. **The tree is byte-identical to how it found it:** 21 of 21 files match their
pre-run sha256, a `diff` of the baseline and final manifests is empty, and no backup was left behind.
Seventeen mutations, each applied alone, run alone, restored and hash-verified **before the next was
written**.

**All eight fix rounds bit.** No row failed to redden.

**And nobody left a patch behind** — with one honest caveat: **nine test and fixture files had no
recorded hash anywhere**, so for those the claim rests on `tsc` exit 0 plus clean prettier and eslint,
which is materially weaker than a hash. A left-behind mutation that no test catches would pass all
three. The brief now requires a hash for every file on an allowlist, tests included.

## 5. What could NOT be verified

- **That Ollama actually compiles the `enum` into a grammar.** There is no model on this machine.
  `grammar.integration.test.ts` is the only instrument that could ever confirm it, and its _passing_
  behaviour is unverified here. What **was** verified: it skips when `RUN_MODEL_TESTS` is unset, and
  it **fails loudly** when set against a closed port — so R-59's silent-skip defect is not reproduced
  in the new marker. Its assertion is also sharper than the obvious one: an enum holding a synthetic
  id absent from the prompt, so a returned citation distinguishes a live grammar from a merely
  obedient model.
- **Whether the nine `RULES` clauses actually steer a 4B model.** The coverage test proves coverage
  and distinctness, not adequacy. Clause 8 is the sharpest instance: token-presence matching cannot
  catch an **inverted** clause, since "make no safety claim" and "make a safety claim" contain the
  same tokens.
- **The `AI_FAKE` echo's construction.** TSD §5.5 fixes what the fake returns, but the echo is built
  by the route, which does not exist until P21. Confirmed: nothing in P19 hardcodes a competing
  shape, `createAiProvider` has no production caller, and `echo` is a generic parameter returned
  verbatim.
- **The refusal rate R-67 predicts.** Measurable only against a real model.
- **That the nine fixtures resemble real `gemma3:4b` output.** They are adversarial-to-a-checklist,
  written from the documents before the implementations existed, and are stated as such rather than
  implied to be sampled reality.

## 6. Deliberately not done

- **`app.ts` is not wired.** M-08 requires exactly one `AiLane` and one `AiProvider` shared by both
  routers, and the property to pin is that the chat and explanation lanes **contend with each
  other** — a test proving two _chat_ requests contend would pass with a lane per router, which is
  the bug. There is no consumer until P20 and P21, so wiring an unused lane now would only add dead
  code. Carried to wave 3 with its test specified.
- **"Process-wide" is realised as "one per app instance."** `createAiLane()` is per-instance, as the
  contract pinned it. Dependency injection cannot express process-wide on its own; what it can
  express is that every consumer shares one lane, and `index.ts` creates exactly one app. That is a
  divergence in **kind** from TSD §5.5's wording and it is stated rather than glossed.
- **Five known denylist gaps** (`curing`, `treating`, `prescribing`, `healthfully`, `doctored`) — see
  R-64.
- **No fifth containment check.** T-19-09's stop-condition override authorises four.
